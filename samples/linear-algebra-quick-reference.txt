Linear Algebra — Quick Reference
=================================

Vectors
-------
A vector is an ordered list of numbers. The dot product of two vectors u and v
measures their alignment:

    u . v = sum_i u_i * v_i = |u| |v| cos(theta)

If the dot product is zero the vectors are orthogonal (perpendicular). The
norm |u| is the square root of u . u.

Matrices
--------
A matrix A of shape (m, n) is a linear map from R^n to R^m. Matrix
multiplication composes these maps, which is why order matters: AB is not
generally equal to BA. The identity matrix I leaves vectors unchanged.

Important quantities:
- Transpose A^T: flip rows and columns.
- Inverse A^-1: exists only for square, full-rank matrices; A A^-1 = I.
- Determinant det(A): zero means the matrix is singular (not invertible) and
  collapses volume.

Linear systems
--------------
Solving A x = b means finding the input that produces b. If A is invertible
the solution is unique: x = A^-1 b. If A is singular there may be no solution
or infinitely many; least squares finds the best approximation when no exact
solution exists.

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
----------------------------
An eigenvector v of A satisfies A v = lambda v: the matrix only stretches it.
Eigenvalues reveal stability (|lambda| < 1 decays) and are central to PCA,
where the top eigenvectors of the covariance matrix define the directions of
maximum variance.

Singular value decomposition
----------------------------
Any matrix can be written as A = U S V^T, where U and V are orthogonal and S
holds the singular values. SVD generalises eigenvalues to rectangular
matrices and powers dimensionality reduction, recommendation systems, and
noise filtering.

Rank
----
The rank of a matrix is the number of linearly independent rows (or columns).
Low rank means the data lives in a lower-dimensional subspace — the key
insight behind compression and latent-factor models.

Practice checklist
------------------
1. Compute a dot product and decide whether two vectors are orthogonal.
2. Determine when a 2x2 matrix is invertible using its determinant.
3. Find eigenvalues of a 2x2 matrix by solving the characteristic polynomial.
4. Explain in one sentence why SVD is useful for compression.
